Editorial management

Editorial Policy & Publication Ethics

Core principles for editorial decisions, peer review and research integrity.

Policy status: This is the establishment-phase framework. The final policy must be formally approved before manuscripts are accepted for publication.

Editorial decision-making

Manuscripts should be assessed for scholarly quality, relevance to the journal scope, originality and methodological soundness. Decisions should not be based on personal characteristics, institutional affiliation or other irrelevant factors.

Peer review

The intended model is external scholarly review with confidentiality throughout the process. Reviewers should provide fair, evidence-based assessments and disclose conflicts of interest.

Research integrity

Originality and plagiarism

Submissions must be original. Suspected plagiarism, duplicate publication or substantial unattributed overlap will be investigated.

Conflicts of interest

Authors, editors and reviewers should disclose interests that could influence assessment or publication.

Corrections

Material errors discovered after publication should be corrected transparently through an appropriate notice.

Retractions

Retraction may be considered where published findings are unreliable or the integrity of the publication process is seriously compromised.

Authorship

All listed authors should have made a meaningful scholarly contribution and approved the submitted version. Changes to authorship after submission require documented agreement from the relevant parties.

Data, images and permissions

Authors are responsible for necessary permissions for third-party material and for accurate reporting of methods and evidence.
