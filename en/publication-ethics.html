Publication Ethics

Publication Ethics

Principles for authors, reviewers and the editorial team.

Originality

Manuscripts must present original scholarly work, with accurate acknowledgment of sources, ideas, data and text.

Multiple submission

A manuscript must not be submitted simultaneously to more than one journal. Duplicate or overlapping publication must be disclosed.

Authorship

Authorship should reflect genuine scholarly contribution, and all listed authors should accept responsibility for the work.

Conflicts of interest

Authors, reviewers and editors should disclose conflicts of interest that could affect scholarly judgment.

Confidentiality

Information about manuscripts under review and review reports must be treated as confidential.

Research integrity

Fabrication, falsification, unjustified manipulation of data or results, and other forms of research misconduct are unacceptable.

Author responsibilities

Authors are responsible for the accuracy and integrity of their work, appropriate citation, respect for third-party rights, and disclosure of funding, conflicts of interest and relevant ethical considerations.

Editorial responsibilities

The editorial team should make scholarly decisions on the basis of research quality and relevance, protect confidential information and handle allegations of misconduct fairly and on the basis of evidence.

Research misconduct

When plagiarism, duplicate publication, fabrication, falsification or another form of misconduct is alleged, the journal may investigate and request explanations or supporting documentation. Further action will depend on the evidence and seriousness of the matter.

Policy basis

The journal will develop and update its publication-ethics policies with reference to recognized international guidance, including the recommendations of the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE).
