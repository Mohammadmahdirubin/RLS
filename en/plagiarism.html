Research Integrity

Plagiarism and Research Integrity

Principles for maintaining originality, transparency and research integrity.

Fundamental principle: Authors bear primary responsibility for the originality and integrity of their submitted work.

Matters subject to review

  • Plagiarism or use of another person's text, ideas, data or results without appropriate acknowledgment
  • Duplicate or substantially overlapping publication without transparent disclosure
  • Simultaneous submission to multiple journals
  • Fabrication or falsification of data and results
  • Unjustified manipulation of data, images, references or results
  • Honorary, ghost or otherwise inappropriate authorship practices

Similarity screening

Where appropriate infrastructure is available, the journal may use specialized text-similarity tools before or during review. A similarity score alone is not treated as conclusive evidence of misconduct; it must be interpreted in context.

Handling procedure

  1. Identification of or report concerning a possible integrity issue.
  2. Preliminary assessment and, where necessary, a request for explanation from the corresponding author.
  3. Review of the available evidence and responses.
  4. Proportionate action based on the evidence and seriousness of the matter.

Post-publication concerns

If a serious integrity problem is identified after publication, the journal may consider a correction, expression of concern or retraction in accordance with its post-publication policy.

Screening tools

The name of any similarity-detection system and any numerical thresholds will be published only after the relevant infrastructure has been formally selected and implemented.
